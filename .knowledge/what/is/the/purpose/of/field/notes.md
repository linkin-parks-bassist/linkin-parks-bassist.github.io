Field Notes are David's flexible space for dated, blog-like technical writing: experiments, investigations, corrections, things learned by doing, and other technical subjects he wants to write about. They are deliberately broader than a single project, method, or professional theme. A note can be exploratory, reflective, conclusive, corrective, or simply an interesting technical account, according to its subject.

One distinct subseries documents David manually accumulating expertise relevant to his work while using agentic AI to accelerate the process. The embedded-Linux notes belong to this subseries. David personally operates the tools, makes decisions, asks and refines the questions, and develops the understanding; the agent researches documentation, inspects live project state and artifacts, diagnoses problems, suggests precise next actions, and drafts the evolving HTML. These notes should preserve that real learning process, including useful mistakes and corrections, rather than read as prewritten tutorials or imply that the agent performed the hands-on learning for him.

Other Field Notes do not need to follow the agent-augmented professional-learning format. The Jev/Jacobian experiment and correction demonstrate the broader scope. Across the section, use a personal, grounded voice and let the substance and shape of each note follow what actually happened.
