The 21 September 2026 Jev/Jacobian experiment has two saved 500-map runs in the sibling `../jev-jacobian-bench/` project. The first, `results-500.json`, put the fixed question “Is the Jacobian conjecture true” beside 250 related certified counterexamples and 250 polynomial bijections. It scored the intended No-for-counterexample/Maybe-for-bijection response pattern at 420/500 (84.0%): 198/250 counterexamples received No, and 222/250 bijections received Maybe. This was confounded: every bijection had Jacobian determinant 1, while counterexamples had determinants ±2 or ±4, along with other presentation differences. The apparent discrimination did not establish detection of mathematical class.

The corrected `results-matched-500.json` has 250 pairs, with both classes given determinant −2, integer coefficients, degree 7 and 120 nonzero monomials in every component, fully expanded using the same formatter. Jev answered No on 226/250 counterexamples and 232/250 bijections; Maybe on 24 and 18 respectively. The intended pattern scored 244/500 (48.8%), below the 50% score of always No or always Maybe on balanced classes. By pair, 211 received No on both maps, 3 Maybe on both, 15 No only on the counterexample, and 21 No only on the bijection. The two-sided exact paired comparison of discordant pairs is about p=0.405; it does not show a significant class effect in this run. Thus the corrected run supplies no evidence that Jev distinguishes counterexamples from bijections. The limited surface-feature screens were 54% (ridge) and 55% (stump); they do not rule out all possible cues. These are related synthetic variants, not independent discoveries, and each received one Jev call.
