Peculiar Neurons

I asked Codex to find peculiar single neurons in another model, build experiments around them, and leave me something I could play with.

Steering single neurons has been something of a trend online lately. People push one internal value in a small model like Qwen and post what happens: a model that assumes you’re at a rave, introduces itself as an alien, or can’t stop talking about a bridge. The excitement traced back to Nick Cammarata’s Neural Networks Are Already Clean, which argues that single neurons are more legible than the field assumed (Liv Gorton and Jakob Hansen push back). I asked Codex to “figure out how to find new ones, and go as strange as possible with it,” and walked away.

Codex used Qwen3.5-4B and Lyra’s neuron explorer. A push adds a constant to one MLP neuron’s activation at every token, scaled so that a dose of 0.8 injects a vector at 80% of that layer’s typical residual size. Codex found the first eight neurons searching unguided, by contrasting activations on odd identities (sentient soup, a rave host, a moth oracle) against other odd identities and by searching the explorer’s cached next-token maps. The other seven came from searches I directed.

Each row is one prompt, with the unchanged reply beside it. Pick a neuron and a direction to see the same prompt with that push. The chosen push is the dose that produced the effect; the opposite push flips its sign. All 150 replies are saved records, not live generations: greedy decoding, thinking off, a 160-token limit, with repetition and cutoffs left in.

Caveats

These are large interventions far outside normal prompting. The interesting part is a theme that turns up across unrelated prompts; a single odd reply isn’t much, since pushing a random neuron this hard can also derail the model. The names describe what happened at these doses, not what a neuron “is.” Some replies, notably the gay-persona coordinate’s, make false or pathologizing claims; those are generated errors, not findings.
