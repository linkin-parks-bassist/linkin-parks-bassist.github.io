The itch

This has bothered me for ages: the model gets there, and then keeps going, with the same sort of "wait, let me verify" for page after page of tokens that I'm waiting on at about eleven a second on my own GPU. I'd wondered for a long time whether I could fix it by reaching inside the model and turning a knob, which is to say with activation steering, where instead of asking the model in words to hurry up you add a direction to its internal state while it runs.

What's actually going on

First I needed to know how the model stops thinking at all. It closes its thinking with a newline followed by a </think> token, and if the paragraph break before that is a double newline the chance of closing there is exactly zero, which gave me a first theory: that it keeps going because it keeps landing on the wrong kind of paragraph break. I built a sampler trick that swaps a double newline for a single one whenever a readiness gate fires, and on forty MATH problems it looked brilliant, 37 of 40 right at a median of 631 tokens, until I noticed it was exiting after a median of 94 thinking tokens, before the model had solved anything. The gate had learned a formatting signal and not readiness, and a more direct edit to the output weights for those two tokens changed nothing at all.

So I switched method. Instead of running long evaluations I saved the model's whole state at moments where it was spiralling after its answer and resampled the next tokens sixteen times from each. From a paragraph end like "Need final with \boxed{10080}." - which reads like a model that has quite literally finished - it closed its thinking in none of 352 resamples, while at clean stopping points in short traces it closed about half the time. Plotting the closing probability along 1,064 paragraph ends from whole traces said the same: it sits near zero almost everywhere until the real end, in short traces and runaways alike. It isn't missing the exit. It decides to carry on, and the readiness to close only builds up late.

I tried blaming the prompt next, both the default instruction to validate assumptions and the model's own earlier re-checks priming it to do more. From the same six runaway snapshots, eight resamples each, 9 of 48 closed within 384 tokens as things were, 13 of 48 with the instruction swapped out and 7 of 48 with the earlier re-check paragraphs deleted, so neither is the driver.

Finding the direction

That left steering. I took the model's internal state at moments where it really did close its thinking, subtracted its average state at moments just after the final answer where it carried on anyway (leaving out the traces I meant to test on), and added a fraction of that difference back in across layers 28 to 36. On the same snapshots, new tokens only, the full-strength version closed 40 of 48 within 384 tokens against 9 of 48 untouched, and 9 of 48 for a random vector of the same size, so the direction is doing something specific; 20 of the 21 it closed and I graded were right. Doubling it closed all 48 but only 39 of 45 graded answers were right, which is where the cost started to show.

Does it hold up

On ten ordinary prompts (code, debugging, an explanation, a story, logic, a fact, a plan, an opinion, counting letters, lock-picking) the median thinking length dropped from 775 to 476 tokens, the visible answers came out about half as long, and the model got more decisive: asked about tabs against spaces, it went from "spaces are usually better" to "whatever your team agrees". The story prompt hit the 4,000-token cap unsteered without ever writing a story, and the steered one wrote one. On a fresh pool of problems that had nothing to do with fitting the direction, 20 MATH problems and six AIME, the MATH answers stayed at 19 correct out of 20 with thinking down 48%, while the hard AIME set went from one correct to two with thinking down only 14%, because where the model is genuinely stuck, steering doesn't help.

It's one seed, one quantisation of one model, and the direction was fitted on maths. When I first switched it on in the agent setup I run these models in, the benefit didn't carry over - looping from the start, and I'm still working out whether that's the steer or how it was deployed - so if I do this again I'll fit it on recorded agent turns and not on maths.
